# Channel notes

Norms change, so check each community's current rules before posting. These notes cover what
reliably matters.

## Show HN (news.ycombinator.com)

- Title: `Show HN: <name> – <what it does, plainly>`. No superlatives, no exclamation marks. Keep it
  under 80 characters.
- Show HN is for things people can try now. Link the repo or a live demo, not a landing page or a
  waitlist.
- The text, or a first comment from the author: what it is, why you built it, how it works, what it
  doesn't do yet, and an honest ask for feedback. Write in the first person, three to six short
  paragraphs.
- Don't ask friends to upvote or comment. Coordinated voting gets posts penalized.

## X / Twitter thread

- Post 1 stands alone: the problem, what you built, and the link. Many readers see only this one.
- Posts 2 to 5: a real output or screenshot, how it works, one limitation, and how to install.
- Keep each post within the character limit. One idea per post. Hashtags are optional, at most one
  or two.

## LinkedIn

- Professional framing: the problem as teams feel it, what changed for you, and what you learned.
- The first two lines show before "see more", so put the problem there.
- Links in the body can reduce reach. Some authors put the link in the first comment, but it's
  fine to put it in the body for clarity.
- 120 to 250 words. No emoji walls.

## Reddit

- Pick subreddits where the tool is on-topic (for example, a language or ecosystem subreddit, or
  r/commandline, r/devops, r/MachineLearning, r/programming). Read each sidebar first. Many
  restrict self-promotion or allow it only on certain days or in certain threads.
- Write it as a post for that community, not an ad. Explain the problem in their terms, and be
  open that you are the author.
- One subreddit at a time, a day or more apart, adapted to each audience. Never cross-post the
  same text everywhere at once.

## Thai developer communities

- Common venues: Facebook groups such as Thai Programmer and Python Thailand, plus language- or
  framework-specific Thai groups, and Blognone (as a news tip, not a self-post). Check each group's
  rules on self-promotion.
- Write natural Thai for Thai developers, not a translation of the English post. Keep technical
  terms, commands, and code in English, as Thai developers use them day to day.
- A tone that works: the problem you hit at work, what you built, an honest limitation, and an
  invitation to try it and open issues. Avoid hard selling.

## GitHub metadata

- Description: the README's first sentence, cut to about 120 characters.
- Topics: 5 to 15 lowercase, hyphenated topics a person would actually search for. Use the
  language, the domain, and the tool category, and drop vanity terms.
- Social preview: 1280×640 PNG, under 1 MB. Set it in Settings → Social preview.

## Social-preview brief (for a designer, or for yourself)

Contents of `launch/social-preview.md`:

- Canvas 1280×640, with a safe margin of about 80 px, since platforms crop the edges.
- Project name, large. A one-line promise of six to ten words. Optionally a small, real snippet of
  output, which reads better than an abstract graphic.
- Two colors plus neutrals, contrast at least 4.5:1, and text readable at a 400 px width.
- No fake UI, no stock imagery, and no logos of other companies.
